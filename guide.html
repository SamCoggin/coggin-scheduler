Start hereWhat the Scheduler is

The Scheduler is the one place that shows who is doing what, on which day, in which van. It reads the jobs from the Trello board and you add the plan: the operatives, the time and the van.

Nothing you do here changes the Trello card itself. The plan is saved on the card in the background, so anyone who opens the Scheduler sees the same plan.

The one ruleA job is planned when it has operatives, a time, and, for transport, travel time and a van. Travel fills itself in from the postcode. Until then it is red or amber and the Scheduler keeps showing it to you.

The boardThree kinds of work

Every day is split into three columns. A job can appear in more than one, because a delivery is prepared in the workshop first.

Production

Work in the workshop before a job goes out. Cleaning, refurbishing, building. It has its own dates and its own operatives.

Transport

Anything with a van: deliveries, collections, site visits, clearances. It needs a driver, travel time and a van.

Labour Operations

Work at our labour: recycling loads in, skips, stock in, a customer collecting. It needs operatives and a time, no van.

The routineYour morning in five steps

Open the week

The Scheduler opens on the current week. Today is outlined in blue.

Read the four numbers at the top

Outstanding, Warnings, Vans on the road, Hours planned. If Outstanding is red there is work to do.

Work down the right-hand column

"Needs a decision" lists every warning and every unplanned job for the week, in day order. Press one and the job opens.

Plan the job

Operatives, van, travel, time window, time. Press Plan it to let the Scheduler fill it in from the items on the card, then change what you know better.

Press Done

The plan is saved on the card. The column shrinks. When the right-hand column says "Nothing needs a decision this week", the week is planned.

The card backPlanning a job

Press any card and it opens. The left side is the facts from Trello: where, the items, the contact. The right side is the plan. Work down it in order.

Operatives

Press a name to put them on the job. Under each name is what they already have that day. "Driver" marks Rob, Jack and Bart. Every transport job needs one of them.

Plan it

The Scheduler works out the standard crew and time from the items on the card: 30 minutes a swivel chair start to finish, 45 for a desk, and so on. Press Plan it and it picks the fewest operatives who can finish in time and sets the time. You can change anything after.

Transported By

Transport only. Our operatives by default. Choose a subcontractor or courier when they do the job with their own vehicle. Leave operatives off and the job is theirs entirely: no van, driver or load from us. Put an operative on as well and they are attending, to hand over keys, meet the truck or sign off. Their time is their time on site, the load stays on the contractor's vehicle, and a van is only needed if they go in ours.

Vehicle

FX75 BKG, FX73 CWF, or Hire. A van marked off the road is struck through. If the load needs more than a Luton the box says so. Choose Hire, type the supplier and reference, and tick Booked once they confirm. It nags from five working days out until you do.

Travel Time

Return travel, labour to the site and back. It comes from real road routing, not a straight line.

  • A few seconds after the board loads, every transport job with a postcode gets its road time from OpenStreetMap routing, plus a fifth for traffic, rounded to five minutes. The block says "Road time from OpenStreetMap routing, 85 miles each way".
  • Until that arrives, or if the routing service is down, it uses an estimate from the postcode area. The block says "Estimated from the postcode area".
  • A time you type is kept for good. The lookup never overwrites it.
  • No postcode on the card means no lookup. Type the time in, or add the postcode to the card.
  • Results are remembered in the browser for 30 days, so each postcode is looked up once.

Rounds

Two or more transport jobs by the same operative on the same day make a Delivery Round, one trip with one travel time.

  • The stops are put in driving order: deliveries before collections, then any cut-off times, then nearest first.
  • The round is routed as one trip, labour to each stop to labour, and the travel is the sum of the real legs plus a fifth for traffic. The round card says "Road time labour to 3 stops and back".
  • Until it is looked up, the round uses the longest single journey plus 20 minutes a stop, and the card says so.
  • The day plan walks the real legs, so the arrival at each stop, any wait for a window, and the time back at Forton follow the road. The Routes view and the routes sheet show each leg's miles.
  • Press the round card to type a different travel time. Yours is kept.

Time Window

When the site can take us. "Arrive after" is the earliest they will let us in. "Finish by" is when the loading bay or building shuts. Leave both blank when any time in the day is fine. The Scheduler then shows the plan in words: leave Forton 8:00, arrive 10:40, back at Forton 14:40.

Van Load

The items on the card added up in cubic metres and kilos against the van. If an item on the card cannot be matched, the load says so and gives no estimate rather than a wrong one. Remove wrong lines with the cross, or add items by hand.

Time

Time per operative on site or in the workshop. Use a quick button or type hours and minutes. "Above standard" appears when the time or crew is more than the items justify. It is not wrong, it should be a decision.

What happened

Once the day has passed, the card back opens with a What happened block at the top. Press Done and change anything that differed: actual time, actual travel, who went, and a note. Or press Did not go and say why. The rail lists past jobs with no record under "to record", and a job that did not go stays in red until the card has a new date. The Manager view shows actual time against the standard, which is how the standards get corrected over time.

Two dates on a deliveryA delivery has a production due date as well as its delivery date. The Scheduler sets it two working days before delivery. Production days are set on the Production card, not the delivery card.

GlossaryWhat the words mean

Unassigned
Nobody is on the job yet.
No site time, No production time, No travel time, No van
What is still missing. The job stays Outstanding until each one is set.
Not QC'd
The workshop has not passed the job for delivery. It shows on the delivery card so you do not send a van for something that is not ready. Trello clears it when the card's checklist is complete, or sign it off on the card back: QC, choose who checked it, Pass QC.
Above standard
More people or more time than the items on the card need. Check it is deliberate.
Delivery Round
Two or more transport jobs by one operative on the same day, in one trip. The travel is the real road time from Forton to each stop and back, looked up from OpenStreetMap routing, plus a fifth for traffic. Until it arrives it is the longest single journey plus 20 minutes a stop. Press the round card to change it.
Customer Collects
The customer or a courier picks up from Forton. It sits in Labour Operations because somebody has to be there to load it. Give it operatives and time like any other labour job, put the time they said they will come in the Collection Slot, and it is flagged if the workshop side is not ready.
Held for sales
A striped card. Sales have asked to hold a date but the customer has not confirmed. It counts against the day so you do not overbook, and it drops off on the date shown if not confirmed. You cannot plan it.
Hire not booked
The job needs a hired vehicle and nobody has ticked Booked yet.
Recycling
Every hour of a working day not allocated to a job. The Scheduler shows it per operative per day and for the week. It is planned work, not spare time.
Standard
The crew and time the items justify. Production: 30 minutes a swivel chair, 10 clean only, 15 storage, 45 a desk. Picking: 15 minutes plus 2 an item.
Working day
8:00 to 4:30 with half an hour for lunch. 7 hours 45 minutes a day, 38 hours 45 minutes a week.

GuardrailsThe rules it checks for you

RuleWhat you see
Company vehicles onlyEvery job goes in a company vehicle, including site visits. Operatives do not use their own cars for work: a private policy does not cover business use, so a trip to a customer's site would be uninsured.
DriversOnly Rob, Jack and Bart drive. A transport job with none of them on it gets a warning.
One van, one driverThe same van on two drivers' jobs on one day is flagged. So is a van that is off the road but still on a job.
Van loadA day or a round over 18.7 cubic metres or 1,000 kg says what vehicle it needs instead, up to a 26 tonne. Never an artic to a site.
City centresManchester, Leeds, Liverpool and the like cap at 18 tonne and need a parking suspension. The vehicle advice says so.
Time windowsA job that cannot finish by its cut-off, or an operative arriving early and waiting, is flagged with the times.
Driver hoursOver 4 hours 30 minutes driving in a day needs a 45 minute break. A driver's day over 11 hours from 8:00 is flagged.
Over capacityAn operative with more than 7 hours 45 minutes on a day shows red in Operative Hours.
If every hold landsA day that would be over if the held dates all confirm is flagged so sales can be warned before they promise it.
Heavy dayA day over its operative hours, or short of vans, gets an amber note naming the lighter days this week and next and the jobs with no fixed window that could move. It suggests, it never moves anything.
Hire lead timeAn unbooked hire within five working days is flagged every day until ticked.

Around the SchedulerThe other views

Day

One day in full: the three columns and every operative's day with what they are on and when a driver is back at Forton.

Month

A pill per day: how many production, transport and labour jobs, leave, and "Over if holds land". Press a day to open it. With Outstanding on, it shows only the days with gaps.

Routes

The week's transport on a map, one colour per day, drawn as rounds from Forton. Below it, each day's rounds with miles, van load and the vehicle advice. Mark a van off the road here. Combine Trips finds jobs booked for later near a stop already going out this week.

Manager

Behind a PIN. Every job with time allocated against its standard, so you can see where time is going.

Loading sheet

Open a job or a round and press Download loading sheet. It is one van on one day. Deliveries are listed in loading order, last drop in first at the front, first drop in last by the tail, each with On and Off tick boxes. Collections come after, because they go on once the deliveries are off, and the sheet shows the load on board after every stop so you can see the fullest point. Then the checks before the van leaves: straps, trolley, load secured, walk-round, fuel, waste transfer notes.

The parts of a job

A job is not one thing. It can have production in the workshop, work on the customer's site, transport, and work at Forton. The card from the CRM says which parts it has and who does each. A clearance a sub-contractor collects has no site work and no van of ours, but the truck comes back to Forton and our operatives unload it, so the Scheduler shows the sub-contractor under Transport for information and a Labour leg on the same day for the unload, with its own operatives and time. A courier delivery gets a labour leg to load the courier. Labour legs sit under Labour Operations and print on the job sheets like any other work.

Time goes in with the first operative

Put the first operative on a job and the standard time for that crew goes in by itself, on production and on transport. Change it if the job needs more or less. If there is no standard to lean on, because the items are not on the card, the Time row turns red until a time is typed, so a job cannot go out with a person and no time.

Recycling tally

Whatever is left of an operative's day after their jobs is recycling, and 100 units is the average for a full day, so each operative has a target for the time they actually had: 3 hours on recycling is a target of about 39. The job sheet carries a Recycling block for the day with the target and 100 boxes to strike through, and the number comes back on the sheet at night. Type it into the Recycling today row at the bottom of the Day view and it is saved to the CRM. The Manager view shows the last four weeks per operative: hours, target, units done and the percentage, amber under 80 percent. A unit is one item of any kind.

Job sheets

On the Day view, press Job sheets. It is one page per operative for that day: their stops in driving order with the time to arrive, the postcode, the contact, the access notes and the items to tick off, then any labour or production work, and an End of day block for start and finish times, van check, anything that did not go to plan, and a signature. Print it in the morning, hand it out, and collect it back at night. The crew use the Trello app and cannot see the plan, so this is what they work from.

Download schedule and Download routes

The download button builds a PDF. On the day, week or month views it is the schedule as a fill-in form: red blanks for anything missing, to be filled in and brought back. On the Routes view it is the map on page one and the stops per day after it, for the driver to take.

Where the jobs come fromCards and Trello

  • Every card on the Jobs Planning Board with a due date is a job. The due date is the day it happens.
  • The label says what the job is and how it moves, in one go: Resale - Delivery/Installation, Clearance - Sub-contractor, Refurb - Collect & Return, Donation - Customer Collects, Job Issue - Redelivery and so on. Courier or Sub-contractor on the label means they are transporting it, not our van.
  • The items list on the card is what the van load and the standards are worked out from. "10 x Mesh Swivel Chair" is read. "Some chairs" is not.
  • A time in the card title, such as "After 13:00 - Example Upholstery", is read into the time window and removed from the name.
  • Annual leave comes from the Holidays and Leave list. Bank holidays for England and Wales are built in: everyone is off and anything booked that day is flagged to move.
  • Someone off sick, on a course, or stuck with a broken van: open the day, press Mark as off next to their name and say why. Their hours drop to nothing for the day and every job they were on is flagged to reassign. Press Back in to undo.
  • Moving a card to a workshop list changes its stage here: Not Started, In progress, Ready.
  • If sales move a card's due date after it was planned, the job shows "Date moved" in red and stays Outstanding until you check the crew, van, travel and window and press Replanned on the card back.
  • The operatives you put on a job become members of the Trello card, so their faces show on it in the Trello app. Take them off and they leave the card. Anyone else on the card stays.
What the Scheduler does not doIt does not move cards, change due dates or write labels. Do that on Trello. Mark sickness with Mark as off in the day view, then move the jobs it flags.
