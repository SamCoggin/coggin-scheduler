F · the full inventory, and what each gets

Views (the top tabs): Day, Week, Month, Routes, Manager. Each is the first step of the trail. The recycling tally is a block inside Day and Manager, not a view, so it has no step.

Popups: the job editor, a person's day, a round, the Day sheet, Timings. All five get the trail in their header and a Back button beside Close.

Chains that can happen: view › day › person › job; view › day › round › job; view › day › job; view › day › Day sheet; view › Timings. The Manager PIN screen is part of the Manager view and needs nothing.

Outside the trail: the card back inside a Trello card is its own page and opens the Scheduler in a new tab, so it starts a fresh trail. The guide and the printed sheets are not layers.

G · the rules

The trail is the view, the day, then each panel in the order it was opened: a person, a round, a job. Any link jumps straight to that layer and drops the layers after it. Back goes one layer up. Close drops the lot and lands on the view, as it does today. The trail is held in the page address, so the browser's own Back button walks it too and a refresh reopens where you were. Nothing about the panels themselves changes: same boxes, same buttons, same saving. The one behaviour change is Open job from a person's day, which today closes the person and opens the job; with the trail the person stays underneath and Back returns to them.
